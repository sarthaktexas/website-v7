about

Currently, I work on the structure and function of complement component 1q-like (C1QL) proteins, which help neurons in the brain form and maintain the chemical junctions between them. My work in the Ressl lab aims to understand how these proteins assemble into the complexes that engage their receptors which are genetically linked to schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and addiction. Because so many approved drugs act on this family of receptors, a structural picture of this interaction could point toward new treatments for psychiatric disorders that remain difficult to manage.

I also collaborate with the Callmann group by using molecular dynamics simulations to determine how a glycopolymer we make can help poorly soluble medicines reach patients. Sugar-based polymers like ours are increasingly explored as drug carriers that could allow lower doses with fewer toxic side effects.

past

Most recently, I worked on the structural biology of phospholipase C epsilon (PLCε), a signaling protein frequently dysregulated in cancers. This work was part of a larger effort in the Falzone lab to understand the regulatory role of phospholipases in cellular signaling pathways and how these mechanisms may eventually be targeted therapeutically.

Before that, I was a software engineer at Hack Club Bank, where I was part of a small team of developers that helped build a financial platform for students and nonprofits to raise and manage finances transparently. Prior to that, I was a designer-developer at Hack Club, where I helped build tools and experiences for students to build projects and learn to code.

I attended the University of Texas at San Antonio, where I earned a BS in biochemistry and focused on biosynthetic organic chemistry and computational biophysics. I am grateful to have gotten my start in research in an organic chemistry group on campus. I also learned small molecule X-ray crystallography from Dr. Hadi Arman at that time. After those experiences, I continued my research career working with Dr. Maria Falzone and Dr. Rahul Mojidra on the structure and function of PLCε using cryo-electron microscopy and fluorescence spectroscopy. For my thesis, I worked on mathematical methods to improve macromolecular structure modeling from cryo-EM density maps. I was co-advised by Dr. Maria Falzone and Dr. Hadi Arman for this work.
